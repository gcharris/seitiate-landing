LEGAL & COMMUNITY

Clear boundaries.
Undecided licence.

Where the engine licence actually stands, and how the project thinks about commercial products, ownership, and portability.

Effective Date: March 24, 2026 · Last Updated: March 24, 2026

Can I contribute to Seitiate?

Not at the moment. The engine repositories are private and there is no public contribution process. We are not accepting external pull requests, and there is no licence under which you could build on the code today.

If you are a builder, developer, or researcher who wants to work with us, get in touch rather than waiting for a repository to open.

What licence is the engine under?

None that you can rely on. Seitiate is proprietary: all rights reserved, no licence granted. The forward licence and publication model are undecided, and undecided is a real answer — it may stay that way.

Earlier versions of this site described the engine as open source under AGPL-3.0. That was never a ratified position and it has been withdrawn. Do not rely on it.

Can I build a commercial product on top of Seitiate?

Only under a separate commercial arrangement. There is no standing licence that permits it. If you have a use case, talk to us and we will tell you whether terms are available.

Charging for managed compute, storage, governance, managed environments, operations, support, or user experience is compatible with the project boundary. The engine and the product experience are not the same thing.

Public doctrine: commercial products should not treat payment for service as a transfer of ownership of the user's content or durable context.

Our position is that products may charge for service and experience without claiming ownership of the person's work. We also reject lock-in through inaccessible derived context. If a hosted product is built on Seitiate, portability and export should remain part of the product boundary rather than being withheld indefinitely behind payment.

What is a current commitment vs. future direction?

The engine's proprietary status is a current legal reality, and so is the fact that its forward licence is undecided. So is the project's public position that users should retain ownership of their content and that portability matters — those commitments hold regardless of what licence the engine ever carries.

By contrast, the exact mechanics of exports, hosted-product safeguards, stronger encryption guarantees, or provenance features may vary by implementation and may still be evolving. Those details should be documented in the relevant product or repository materials rather than assumed from this page alone.

What still needs legal review?

Hosted-product contracts, data-processing terms, enterprise security representations, and any product-specific ownership or portability language should still receive formal legal review before being treated as finalized public commitments.
