Five Architectural Boundaries for Partner Module Evaluation

When evaluating partner modules across Startuplan, VisiWatt, and Sakme Office, each component is assessed against five architectural boundaries to determine whether it runs best as a partner-hosted service over the HTTP seam or is selectively integrated into Sakme Office:

  1. API & Event Seam: What exact HTTP endpoints, webhooks, or evidence.v1 receipts does the module consume and emit?
  2. Persisted State & Tenancy: Which tables hold tenant data, and how is per-person tenant isolation enforced?
  3. Authorization & Approvals: Which actions are read-only vs. gated by an owner approval tap?
  4. Portability & Export: How does a customer export their BOM, revisions, or site AST cleanly?
  5. Accountable Maintainer & IP Boundary: Who maintains the service in production, keeping hosted-use rights strictly distinct from any explicit code-reuse permission?

Two-Direction Commercial Accounting, Kept Strictly Apart

Our commercial design keeps direct supplier purchases and partner room customer earnings in two separate ledgers (never netted in code):

  • Platform Purchases from Partner: Direct platform use of partner services (such as Hooshi voice transcription capacity, xRusted site hosting, or integration work) is accounted for as direct supplier usage.
  • Partner Earnings Through the Platform: Customer subscriptions and metered SEIT credit usage inside partner rooms or vertical packs follow configurable per-room commercial settings, supporting both standard platform revenue-sharing and zero-fee partner cooperation modes.
